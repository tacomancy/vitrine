Feature splitting in SAE dictionaries
status: openstarted: 2026-08-19anchors: 4
Training a wider dictionary on the same activations does not simply give more features — it gives narrower ones. A single latent that fired on "legal citations" becomes three latents: US case law, statute references, and bluebook formatting.
The question is whether splitting is a real property of the model's representation or an artifact of the sparsity penalty at a given width.
Evidence for artifact: the split components are not independently steerable, and ablating any one of them degrades the others' activation in a way that tracks dictionary width rather than semantics.
L0 ≈ 22 @ width 2^14  ·  L0 ≈ 19 @ width 2^16
See [[Monosemanticity — working definition]] for the criterion I'm using, and @Bricken2023 §4 for the original observation.

p.14 / 61
4  Feature Splitting
As we increase the size of the learned dictionary, features which were previously represented by a single latent are pulled apart into several latents which fire on narrower contexts.
This raises a question we do not resolve here: whether the finer features are present in the model and merely unresolved at smaller dictionary sizes, or whether the decomposition is imposing them.
We find the phenomenon is robust across dictionary sizes and random seeds, though the specific split points vary.
