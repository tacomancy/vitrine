# 0017: The Lexicon is derived from the text of a Tag's papers, with seeds and exclusions

**Status:** Accepted

The brief (`design-brief.md` § The tag lexicon) builds each Tag's Lexicon from "the author-supplied keywords" on accepted papers. Research #132 found that of the five Structured sources only PubMed returns author keywords, and only for biomedicine; for the CS/ML literature the brief's own examples come from, the Lexicon as written has no input. This ADR, from wayfinder ticket #144, decides what a Lexicon is built from instead. The shape: a Tag's Lexicon is **derived from the titles and abstracts of the Tag's papers** — phrases that recur across them and are rare across the rest of the vault — with **author keywords as extra votes when a source happens to supply them**, and the user's **seeds and exclusions** applied on top; it is **one term set with one compiler per Structured source**, so the attention scatter and a Scout's Filter measure and search the same meaning of a Tag. The observation that makes this the right substitute rather than a compromise: both consumers — OpenAlex's `title_and_abstract.search` and arXiv's `search_query` — search title and abstract text, so terms *taken from* that text are the ones that retrieve; author keywords were only ever a proxy for it. Constants and table shapes are `docs/architecture.md` § Lexicon.

## Decisions

1. **A Lexicon is derived from the text of a Tag's papers.** A **Term** is a phrase of one to three words from the title or abstract of the Tag's papers. It counts only if it recurs in at least two of them — one abstract's phrasing is noise — and is scored by in-Tag recurrence discounted by how common the phrase is across *all* the vault's papers, so "we show" and the vault's own house vocabulary fall away and a child Tag's Terms are the ones that separate it from its siblings and parent, which is what makes the brief's "flat, not inherited" hold in practice. A stopword list applies. The top K Terms are the Lexicon; the rest are visible below the line in the editor as *not used*. No model call: the Lexicon is reproducible from the vault, works before BYOK (beat 7) lands, and a Term the user cannot see the reason for is exactly the "quietly attached to the wrong keywords" failure the brief warns about. PDF-read keywords and OpenAlex's machine vocabulary were considered (below).
2. **The Tag's papers are the same set as its Coverage and its point on the scatter.** A Source or Source stub votes for a Tag if it carries the Tag itself (frontmatter or inline) or is explicitly attached to a Question carrying it — a Question's Related, a Research Question's supporting or opposing lists, or an accepted stub's `origin_question` (ADR 0016 d.9). The scatter's "my coverage" axis and point size are already "my paper count for that tag"; deriving the Lexicon from any other set would make a point and its label disagree. A parent's papers are the union of every descendant's, as Coverage already rolls up; "flat, not inherited" holds in the other direction only — a child never takes its parent's Terms. A leaf with too few papers is unplaced at its depth and still counts toward its parent, which is the brief's "zooming out reveals it".
3. **Author keywords are extra votes when known, never the mechanism.** `keywords:` on a Source stays, meaning *author-supplied, when known*: a Structured source that returns them (PubMed) fills it at accept; the user may type them. An author keyword skips the two-paper recurrence floor — the authors chose it deliberately, it is not incidental phrasing — but not the vault-wide discount or the top-K cut, so "machine learning" does not place every Tag in the same spot. Keywords printed in the PDF under the abstract are read at Ingest into App state (the Source's sidecar), never written into the user's frontmatter — the app rewrites owned `##` sections after creation, not frontmatter — and that reading is its own small slice *after* beat 10, not part of beat 5's Ingest, which already carries re-matching, `Q:`, and the Unmatched panel.
4. **Derived terms are a projection; the user's edits are overrides.** The derived set is recomputed from the vault whenever the Index changes and lives in `index.sqlite` (disposable, ADR 0014). The user's edits are recorded as **Excluded** Terms (never derived back for this Tag) and **Seeds** (always included), and only those live in `lexicon.json` — exactly what cannot be rebuilt. This keeps "state is derived, not declared" and keeps the brief's "visible and editable" from turning every Tag into a configuration screen after day one. Seeding is typed Terms only; "point a tag at a few papers" is ordinary tagging — the Source joins the Tag's papers under decision 2 — so there is no second, invisible way for a Source to belong to a Tag.
5. **One term set, one compiler per Structured source, OR of quoted phrases.** The Lexicon is the Tag's meaning; the query syntax is the source's. The top-K Terms compile to an OR of quoted phrases — a Term is the Tag in different words, any of them is a hit; an AND would be nearly empty — as `title_and_abstract.search` for OpenAlex's growth-rate count and as `abs:`/`ti:` clauses for an arXiv Scout whose Filter names a Tag. The editor shows each compiled query verbatim: the cheapest possible form of "each tag shows what it is measured as". The compilers are separate things; the set is not.
6. **Unplaced keeps its meaning; its condition is a count.** A Tag is *unplaced* when fewer than N of its papers have text behind them and it has no Seed; a seeded Tag is placed at any count. Never shown as low. The paper count sits on every point, as the brief already asks.
7. **A measurement remembers the query it was made with.** Under decision 4 the derived set shifts with every accept, but a growth-rate series was fetched for one compiled query. Each measurement stores that query; a Tag whose current query differs from its measured one is shown as *re-measure pending* — distinct from unplaced and from low — until the next sync re-measures it. The chart says what it measured, never what it would measure now; the sync cadence and the visual are beat 10's spec.
8. **The Lexicon lands with beat 10; Tags join the Filter in a short slice after it.** The scatter is the consumer that cannot exist without it, and the editor and the point it explains are designed together. A Scout already has a working Query (ADR 0016 d.2), so Tags-in-Filter is one arXiv compiler over an existing term set, sequenced after beat 10.
9. **A Proposal keeps its keywords field; the card omits a row a source is known never to supply.** "Missing rather than guessed" is about not fabricating; *keywords — none* on every arXiv card is noise, not honesty. This narrows `docs/architecture.md` § Scouts' "the card shows the field as missing" for arXiv.

## Considered options

- **Keywords read from the PDF as the mechanism.** Rejected, decision 1: only papers with an attached PDF *and* a venue that prints keywords would contribute; stubs, which are most of accept history, contribute nothing. Kept as extra votes, decision 3.
- **OpenAlex `keywords`/`topics` shown as machine vocabulary.** Rejected: a Lexicon measured against OpenAlex must not be *built from* OpenAlex, or the scatter measures OpenAlex's classifier against itself; and it is a second API call per accepted paper.
- **Manual seeding as the primary path.** Rejected: "no configuration screen" is the brief's point. Seeds stay the fallback, decision 4.
- **A model call to propose Terms.** Rejected, decision 1. A clearly-labelled *suggestion* path could be added later; it is not the mechanism.
- **Snapshot-then-edit** (derive once, then a plain editable list). Rejected, decision 4.
- **A separate "seed papers" list.** Rejected, decision 4.
- **Two term sets, one per consumer; AND-compilation.** Rejected, decision 5.
- **Landing the Lexicon with the Scout beat so Filters get Tags sooner.** Rejected, decision 8.
- **Re-fetching growth rate on every accept; keeping old numbers silently.** Rejected, decision 7 — the first churns the OpenAlex budget, the second makes the chart lie.

## Consequences

- **+** The Lexicon exists for every Tag with enough papers on the day beat 10 lands, from the vault alone: no key, no model, no second API.
- **+** `lexicon.json` holds only seeds and exclusions; everything else rebuilds with the Index.
- **+** The scatter and a Scout's Filter cannot disagree about what a Tag means.
- **−** The brief's wording — "author-supplied keywords" as the vote — is revised; `CONTEXT.md` § Lexicon, Term, Seed, Excluded, Tag's papers, Unplaced carry the new meaning, and `keywords:` on a Source narrows to "when known".
- **−** Derived phrases are text statistics over a small corpus; a Tag with three papers has a thin Lexicon, and the editor must make that legible (paper count, *not used* line) rather than hide it.
- **−** The measurement row gains a stored-query column that beat 10 must design around from the start.
- **−** Seeds and exclusions are keyed by Tag; tag hierarchy management is out of scope by the brief, so a renamed Tag starts over.
