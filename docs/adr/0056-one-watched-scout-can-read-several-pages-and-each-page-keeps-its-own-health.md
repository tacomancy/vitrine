# 0056: One Watched Scout can read several pages, and each page keeps its own health

**Status:** Accepted

A researcher watching one lab across its *research*, *news* and *blog* pages needs a Scout per page today, each with its own name, cadence, Lane and Assigned Questions to keep in step, and its own Accept rate to judge. The brief says a Scout "watches one source" (`## Primary objects`, `## Scouts`), and `CONTEXT.md` **Watched source** is one URL (ADR 0043). A grill on 2026-10-28 settled that one Watched Scout may read several pages. It is hard to reverse (the Scout file's shape, a queue column, and every figure's choice between the Scout and a page), it diverges from the frozen brief, and it had real alternatives. The vocabulary is `CONTEXT.md` § Scouts; the shape is `docs/architecture.md` § Scouts, § BYOK and watched sources and § The Scouts screen.

## Decisions

1. **One Scout, several pages.** A Watched Scout has one or more Watched sources, each one URL, read on its own. Its name, cadence, starting Lane, Assigned Questions, Cap and aging windows are the Scout's, as are its Queue entry and its Accept rate. A Structured source stays one source per Scout. This amends the brief's "watches one source" to *watches one Structured source, or one or more Watched sources*.
2. **Any Watched sources mix; Structured sources never do.** A page with a Feed and a page that needs an Extraction can sit in one Scout, since whether a page advertises a feed is found at every fetch (ADR 0040 decision 3) and is not a kind. A page and an arXiv or OpenAlex source cannot: a Structured source carries a Filter, a date window and *also search back to*, and a different kind of source is a new Scout (ADR 0043 decision 1). A Scout whose every page is a feed still says *no model call*.
3. **A run is one page; a check is the runs one moment made.** A check writes one `scout_runs` row per page it reads, each with its page in `query` as today, tied together by a nullable `check_id`; an older row's is null and stands as a check of its own. ADR 0043's predicate is unchanged — a run is at page P when its `query` is P — so every page-scoped read keeps its meaning. *Last run*, *Run now*, cost per run and the run list read the check.
4. **Due is per page.** A page is due when its own last completed run is a cadence old, or when it has none; a check runs the Scout's pages that are due then, and a page not yet due is not fetched early. *Run now* on the Scout runs every page as one check.
5. **Each page has its own health; the Scout's Voice is the worst of its pages'.** Each page's Voice and Warrant are derived from its own runs exactly as a one-page Scout's are now, its *usually ~N a week* and its *no baseline yet* included. The Scout's Voice takes the worst page's (*wrong*, then *not yet*, then *claim*) and, when it is not *claim*, names the pages that are not, with each page's own sentence verbatim (ADR 0032 decision 7). A Quiet field is a claim about a page, never about the Scout: a page that parses cleanly and has gone quiet must never be hidden by a sibling that keeps producing, since *last new item* read across the Scout would advance and make it a Quiet field nobody saw (`## Scouts` § Source health). So a multi-page Scout's row on the Scouts screen always lists its pages, each with its own Voice line.
6. **Rows about a broken page name the page.** Loose Ends and Home draw one row per broken page, naming the Scout and the page: ADR 0040 decision 7's *one row per Scout* becomes one per page, and *structure change detected* is judged per page. The Queue rail's marker follows the Scout's Voice, and its *Needs you* line names the page. *Run now* on a page's row runs that page only, so mending one page never spends on the others unasked (ADR 0040 decision 5); *open the page* opens it. Pause stays the Scout's: there is no paused page, and a page that should not be read is removed.
7. **Adding, removing and changing a page are ADR 0043 per page.** An added page is a first look at that page alone: due at the next check, its first run proposing everything it lists, all Retroactive, its own *reject this run*; saving runs nothing. A removed page loses nothing: its runs, Proposals, triage rows and stubs stay the Scout's, so its Accept rate, cost and Candidates proposed keep them, and only its page-scoped figures stop being drawn; adding it back takes its runs again. An edited address is a removal and an addition, by the trimmed string. ADR 0043's *changed page* is the one-page case of this rule. A Scout has at least one page.
8. **The Accept rate stays the Scout's, with a breakdown by page.** The headline and the weekly line read every page, past and present. An opened row lists each page's rate, credited by its first Appearance's run's `query` — the first-Appearance credit `acceptCounts` already gives — as a plain list with no line, colour or order of merit, so *remove this page* can be decided on evidence. The *unavailable* guard applies to the headline and to each page apart. ADR 0043 decision 8's sentence becomes *a page was added or removed* within the twelve weeks.
9. **Cost per run is per check, with a mean per page.** A check's cost is its rows' sum; *Cost / run* is the mean over the trailing thirty days' checks that called a model; *each run* lists checks with their pages beneath; the opened row also gives each page's mean, so one costly page can be found. *Unpriced* and *no key* rows are treated as now, row by row.
10. **Every per-run number is per page, and the number of pages is not capped.** The 25 Item pages, the 40K reduction, the 500 feed entries, the timeout and the response cap apply to each page's run. A Scout of ten pages costs about ten times one of a page, and *try* says so before Save; as with spend (ADR 0040 decision 6), a cap on pages would be a number the app cannot defend.
11. **One item on two of a Scout's pages is one card and no Corroboration.** The Source key merges it and its Item page is read once. Corroboration counts distinct Scouts, and two pages of one Scout are not independent finds; Candidates proposed counts it once. An item a newly added page lists that the Scout already holds is not new, so it is not in that page's Retroactive batch.
12. **The file says `url:` or `urls:`, and nothing is migrated.** `source: { kind: watched, url }` is a one-page Scout, as every file is now; `source: { kind: watched, urls: [ … ] }` is a list of one or more, unique once trimmed. A file with both, or an empty list, cannot be read and says why. No file is rewritten to change its shape (ADR 0009, ADR 0016 decision 4): a save that leaves one page keeps `url:`, and one that gives it a second writes `urls:` in place, comments and unknown keys kept.
13. **The form lists Addresses under one *try*.** In *What it watches*, *Watching* offers *web pages*; *Addresses* is one group row of content-width inputs, each with a secondary *remove* and an *add another address* under the last, *remove* disabled beside *A Scout needs at least one page.* while one is left; a duplicate is refused beside the second copy. One *try* tries every page — its listing and up to three Item pages, as now — and draws each result under its own address, a page that fails in its own sentence beside the others' results, then one summed projection, *≈ $X for the first look at N pages*. The note: *Each page is read on its own and has its own health. Adding a page runs nothing; its first look proposes everything it lists.* The draft keeps the list.

## Considered options

- **A group object over Scouts.** Rejected, decision 1: a second object whose name, Assigned Questions and cadence must stay in step with its members', and the rail and the Accept rate would each have to choose between the group and its Scouts. Decision 1 is the same thing in fewer parts.
- **The form makes one Scout per address.** Rejected: the cheapest and reversible, but it leaves the researcher N Scouts to edit, pause and judge, which is the request.
- **One run row per Scout check, with a child table of pages.** Rejected, decision 3: every page-scoped read ADR 0043 lists, the Voice and the scheduler would move onto the child table, where one row per page needs none of them changed.
- **One Voice for the Scout with no lines per page.** Rejected, decision 5: a productive page beside a dead one is the failure the brief calls the most dangerous.
- **Pausing a page.** Rejected, decision 6: a second kind of *not looking* that coverage gaps and the fleet claim would have to learn, when removing the page does it and keeps its history.
- **Run now on a page row runs the Scout.** Rejected, decision 6: it spends on pages the researcher did not ask about.
- **Always `urls:`, with a migration of every file.** Rejected, decision 12: a rewrite of files the researcher wrote, for a key's shape.
- **Counting two pages of one Scout as Corroboration.** Rejected, decision 11: they are one brief's finds, not two.
- **A cap on pages per Scout.** Rejected, decision 10.
- **A *try* per address.** Rejected, decision 13: the number wanted before Save is the total, and one *try* is the form's grammar now.

## Consequences

- **+** One lab across its pages is one Scout to name, assign, pause and judge.
- **+** Every page still has the health a one-page Scout has, and a quiet page can never hide behind a busy one.
- **+** No file is migrated, and the page-scoped reads ADR 0043 made keep their meaning.
- **−** Every figure now has three readings — the Scout's, a page's, a check's — and one added later must choose; decisions 3, 5, 8 and 9 are how.
- **−** Two Scouts folded into one lose the Corroboration between them, so Rank's second signal goes quiet for the papers both found.
- **−** A Scout with many pages has a tall row on the Scouts screen, since its pages are always listed.
- **−** A first look at several pages costs their sum, shown on *try* and uncapped.
- **−** `CONTEXT.md` **Scout**, **Watched source**, **Due**, **Retroactive**, **Source health**, **Quiet field**, **Accept rate**, **Cost per run** and **Corroboration** widen, and ADRs 0016, 0017, 0040, 0042, 0043 and 0055 take dated Updates.
- The spec carries: the file's two forms; `check_id` and per-page Due; the page and Scout Voices and the Scouts screen's page lines; the form's Addresses and its *try*; the per-page rows on Loose Ends, Home and the rail, and *run now* on a page; the Accept-rate breakdown and its sentence; cost per check; and the closing demo.
