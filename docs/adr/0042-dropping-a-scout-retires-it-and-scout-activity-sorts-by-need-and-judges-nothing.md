# 0042: Dropping a Scout retires it, and Scout Activity sorts by need and judges nothing

**Status:** Accepted

Beat 9 (#176) is the Scout Activity dashboard (`design-brief.md` § Scout Activity, Prompt 10) and DEL-10, *the row acts* (#251). Its issue leaves one call to the spec — what becomes of a dropped Scout's runs and pending Proposals — and a grill on 2026-10-06 closed the others the brief and prototype 10 left open. Every decision below is hard to reverse once a Scout has been dropped, once a rate has been charted, or once a row has been painted amber, and each has a plausible alternative a later reader would propose. The Voice assignment and the Warrant are ADR 0032's and are not reopened.

## Decisions

1. **Drop retires; it never erases.** The Scout's file gains a `dropped: <date>`, the scheduler skips it, and the Queue rail and Scout Activity stop listing it. Its runs, its `triage` rows and the stubs that name it in `origin_scout` all stay, so health, Accept rate and *Clocked but unquestioned* (which reads the Scout's Assigned Questions) keep reading true. Its pending and deferred Proposals stay in Review until triaged, under the Scout's name, marked dropped. None is rejected for the user: a bulk reject writes `triage` rows that would be read as a verdict on a brief the user has already given up on. Undoable while the row is on screen, and afterwards from the one line that lists dropped Scouts (decision 9).
2. **Accept rate over time excludes one kind of row.** A *reject this run* whose run was Retroactive (`triage.batch` set, run `retroactive`) is not counted: it clears a backward search nobody wanted and says nothing about the brief (ADR 0039 d.5 left the call to this beat). A *reject this run* on an ordinary run still counts. The series is one point per week over the trailing twelve, drawn only for a week with at least five triaged Review items; a thinner week is a gap and never a zero, and each point carries its `n`.
3. **Which fields make the rate unavailable follows ADR 0018 d.9.** The ≥ 50 % guard (`docs/architecture.md` § Scouts) tests only the fields the source kind is expected to supply: `authors` for arXiv, `authors` and `venue` for a Watched source. arXiv carries a venue only when `journal_ref` is present, so counting it missing would have made every arXiv Scout's rate unavailable on the day this ships. The sentence stating why stays; it is still a fact about the measure and never a health state.
4. **A coverage gap is an open Map row with no Scout looking for it.** Rows are the Question Map's, so one inquiry is never two. A Scout covers only while it is unpaused, undropped and not blocked on a missing key; a *broken* one still covers, since its fault surfaces elsewhere. A Question whose only Scouts are not looking is listed with them named. The list is cut with the cut stated and never totalled; *brief a scout* opens the new-Scout form with the Question Assigned. When there are no gaps the block is a claim, warranted by what it checked and naming any Scout not looking (ADR 0032 d.8).
5. **The row edits the Scout's real fields, and the dry run is arXiv-only.** A Scout has no prose brief; *edit* expands the row to the Query and Assigned Questions the form already writes, through `scouts.save`. A *try over the last run's window* compares `totalResults` with that run's `fetched`, for arXiv alone — the prototype's *would have proposed 23 instead of 214*. It is its own ticket so it can slip without blocking the rest. Editing a Watched Scout's address stays on the form: a changed page invalidates `page_hash` and the structure-change test, which is its own decision.
6. **Cost is per run, and a run that did not look is not a run.** One figure per Scout, *cost / run*, the mean over the trailing thirty days' runs that made a model call; a per-run list sits behind the row. An arXiv Scout says *no model call*, never `$0.00`; a null price shows tokens and *unpriced*. A `no key` run is a row that fetched nothing (ADR 0040) and is excluded from every count of runs, from the mean, and from the Quiet-field baseline.
7. **Queue depth and age are derived in the core, for Home too.** Pending Review rows per Scout and fleet-wide, deferred counted apart, age as the median and oldest `first_seen`, stated as neutral facts with no colour. Skim is named as having no depth. Home (beat 12) renders this derivation and does not recompute it.
8. **Rows are sorted by need and carry no judgement colour.** Faults first (broken, key rejected), then Scouts not looking, then Accept rate ascending, with *unavailable* and *nothing triaged* last; columns sort. No rate is painted, no row is pre-expanded, and *edit* has the same weight on every row. Prototype 10's amber 9 % needs a threshold the app cannot defend, the same objection as ADR 0032 d.5; the worst brief rising to the top, with its trend drawn, carries the case. A threshold remains a decision for a later ADR.
9. **Dropped Scouts live on one quiet line.** *2 dropped* at the foot of the table, opening to the names with *restore* on each, absent when none. Restoring clears `dropped`; the Scout resumes on its next due check and its Proposals were never touched.
10. **Candidates proposed is a 30-day count credited by first Appearance.** The same credit rule `acceptCounts` uses, so the two cannot disagree. A Held Proposal is excluded and counted as *N already in your vault*; a card another Scout also found shows *N also found elsewhere*. No second chart: proposals per week is an accumulation statistic.
11. **No Loose Ends row and no new Home item.** A coverage gap is worth knowing, not broken, and a dropped Scout's Proposals are a choice. Beat 9 owes Home only the derivation in decision 7.

## Considered options

- **Delete the file and orphan the rows.** Rejected, decision 1: `origin_scout` and *Clocked but unquestioned* would dangle, and it cannot be undone.
- **Retire and also clear its Proposals.** Rejected, decision 1: a rejection nobody made, written into the Accept-rate record.
- **Count every *reject this run*; or exclude every one.** Rejected, decision 2: the first lets a cleared backfill read as a bad brief, the second discards a real judgement that a run was junk.
- **Paint a poor rate amber and pre-expand it, as the prototype does.** Rejected, decision 8.
- **A proposals-per-week chart.** Rejected, decision 10.

## Consequences

- **+** Drop is as reversible as pause, and nothing the app wrote ever points at nothing.
- **+** Accept rate, queue depth and the fault sentence each have one derivation that Scout Activity, Home and Loose Ends share.
- **−** A dropped Scout's Proposals sit in Review until the user clears them, so a dropped Scout can keep a stack alive; the stack says whose it was.
- **−** With no threshold, a bad brief is found by reading the top of the sort, not by being flagged. If that proves too quiet, the remedy is an explicit threshold ADR, not a tint.
- **−** The weekly series is gappy for a low-volume Scout, which is true.
- The spec carries: the `dropped` key and its readers (scheduler, rail, `scouts.list`), the weekly accept-rate series and the Retroactive-batch exclusion, the corrected missing-field guard, the coverage-gap derivation, per-run cost totals, queue depth and age, the sorted table with in-place edit, cadence and pause, drop with its undo and restore line, the line chart in `charts/` (D3 scales, class-name colours, #141), and — as a separable ticket — the arXiv dry run.
