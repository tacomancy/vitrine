Direction

Brass, glass, and a dark room

A vitrine is a display case: brass frame, dark interior, one light raking across the glass. That is the whole system in a sentence. The ground is a blue-black you could stare into for eight hours. The jewels — sapphire, emerald, garnet, amethyst — carry meaning and identity. Brass is the light, and light is scarce on purpose.

Dark is the default

Light mode exists and is fully audited, but the brand is built dark-first. The cream "playbill" mode is a 1920s printed program, not a bleached copy of the dark UI.

Brass is punctuation

The focus ring, the active nav item, a number worth noticing, the wordmark. If more than roughly 5% of a screen is brass, it has stopped being precious.

Sapphire does the work

Every button, link, and selected state is sapphire. Gold buttons look like a scam; blue buttons look like software.

Deco is geometry, not ornament

Stepped corners, hairline double rules, wide letterspaced caps, symmetry. No filigree, no fan motifs scattered as decoration, no drop shadows pretending to be gilding.

One assumption to correct if it's wrong: I've written the positioning for Vitrine as a tool for presenting and curating work — that's what the name suggests. The color, type, and token systems don't depend on it, but the voice notes and the sample copy do. Tell me what Vitrine actually does and I'll re-cut those.

Color · ramps

Eight families, built in OKLCH

Every step is generated at a fixed perceptual lightness, so a 500 in one family is exactly as light as a 500 in any other. That is what makes the semantic mappings below swappable. Click any swatch to copy its hex.

Emerald

Brass

The accent. Focus rings, emphasis, the wordmark. Never a default button.

Copper

Warm accent. Warning, caution, the second metal.

Garnet

Danger. Jewel-toned, so it scolds without shouting.

Amethyst

Extended. Chart identity, tags, the sixth voice.

Teal

Decorative only — too low-chroma in sRGB to hold a chart slot.

Ink

Blue-black neutrals. Every surface and every line.

Typography

One face with a past, one with a job

Josefin Sans is a 1920s geometric revival — beautiful at 40px, unreadable at 13. Inter is the opposite. The rule is simple: Josefin never appears below 20px and never inside the product UI.

Marks

Three readings of a display case

All three survive at 16px and all three work as a square app icon. The Case is my recommendation — it is the only one that carries the name's meaning rather than just its letter.

The Case · recommended

A stepped Deco cabinet on a plinth, lit glass inside, the V cut as the light itself. Reads as an object, not a letter.

The Rake

Three nested chevrons under a hairline — Deco speed-lines, and the V of Vitrine. The most typographic of the three; the best fit if the product is fast.

The Facet

A beveled octagonal pane with the V as the bevel catching light. The softest of the three, and the one that scales smallest cleanly.

Lockups

Clearspace on all sides equals the height of the plinth (⅒ of the mark). Minimum mark size 16px; minimum horizontal lockup width 104px — below that, drop to the mark alone.

Don't recolor the glass

The inner pane is always ink or sapphire-800. A green or red pane turns the case into a traffic light.

Don't add a gradient sheen

The brass gradient is already in the mark. A second highlight sweep reads as 2009 web design, not 1929 metalwork.

Don't set the wordmark in Inter

The wordmark is Josefin Sans 300 at 0.20em tracking. Always. It is the only place that tracking value is used.

Don't put the mark on a busy photo

If there's no flat ground, use the one-color version inside a solid ink plate with the standard clearspace.

Data color

Six voices, in a fixed order

Series colors are assigned in slot order and never cycled or reshuffled — a filter that removes series 3 must not repaint the rest. These six clear a worst adjacent-pair separation of ΔE 11.4 under simulated protanopia and deuteranopia, against a target of 8, in both modes. They're identical in light and dark, so a screenshot survives a mode switch.

Slot order, demonstrated

Example values — each line is one palette slot
1. Sapphire2. Emerald3. Brass4. Garnet5. Amethyst6. Copper

Sequential is one hue

Sapphire, light→dark in the playbill mode and dark→light in the dark mode. Never a rainbow ramp for magnitude.

Diverging is garnet ↔ emerald

With a neutral ink-300 midpoint. Never a jewel hue at the midpoint — that reads as a third category.

Status never borrows a slot

Series colors live on the 500/600 steps; status lives on 300 (dark) and 700 (light). They share families but never a step — and status always ships with an icon and a label, never color alone.

Seven series is too many

There is no slot 7. A seventh entity folds into "Other", or the chart becomes small multiples. Teal is in the ramps but can't hold a slot — sRGB has no chroma left at that lightness.
